## Per-lesson findings

- Day 19 introduces the scalar chain rule on a minimal composition, y = (3x + 1)^2, and requires an expansion check (9x^2 + 6x + 1 differentiates to the identical 18x + 6) before any drill. All numeric claims were independently recomputed: d/dx (3x + 1)^2 = 24 at x = 1; d/dx (2x - 1)^3 = 6 at x = 1; d/dx (5x - 2)^3 = 135 at x = 1 (expansion 375x^2 - 300x + 60 agrees); d/dx (x^2 + 1)^2 = 40 at x = 2; d/dx (2x^2 + 1)^3 = 108 at x = 1; d/dx (4x + 3)^2 = 24 at x = 0. The dropped-inner-derivative error is quantified (loses factor 5, then factor 4), and the kink case |2x - 2| at x = 1 is shown to have left slope -2 and right slope +2, correctly attributed to the differentiability hypothesis of the chain rule.
- Day 20 builds partial derivatives by freezing variables, assembles the gradient, and verifies the steepest-ascent property numerically. Recomputed: for f(x, y) = x^2 y + 3y - x at (2, 1), f = 5, ∂f/∂x = 3, ∂f/∂y = 7; for f = x y^2 at (3, 2), (4, 12) with f = 12; for f = x^2 + y^2 at (3, 4), gradient (6, 8), norm 10, unit (0.6, 0.8), and a directional difference quotient of about 10.0001 matching the norm; for f = xy + y^2 at (1, 2), gradient (2, 5), norm sqrt(29) ≈ 5.3852; for the path rule, z = xy with x = t^2, y = 3t gives 9t^2 = 36 at t = 2, identical to direct substitution into 3t^3; the shared-variable case L = a*b with b = a + 1 yields 2a + 1 = 7 at a = 3 against the single-path error of 4; the mastery check f = x^2 + 2y^2 at (1, 1) gives gradient (2, 4), norm 2*sqrt(5) ≈ 4.4721; and |x| + |y| is correctly shown to lack a gradient at the origin.
- Day 21 is the scheduled review day (`is_review_day: true`). The forward/backward pass L = (a*b + c)^2 at (2, 3, 1) produces n1 = 6, n2 = 7, L = 49 and input gradients 42, 28, 14, each confirmed by central finite differences; this is exactly the micrograd-engine computation the later autograd project imitates. The shared-node activity proves contributions add (2u = 6 for L = (a + 1)^2 at a = 2; 24 for w = (u + v)^2 with u = x^2, v = 2x at x = 1). The retrieval harness re-verifies the block's numbers (24, 135, 40, 3, 7, 10, 42, 28, 14). The final assessment uses L = (a*b)^2 + c at (1, 2, 3) giving L = 7 with gradients 8, 4, 1, and the ReLU kink correctly distinguishes "undefined at 0" from the conventional subgradient 0 used by engines.
